<Panel>
**A car accident settlement is your economic damages (medical bills, lost wages,
and vehicle damage) plus pain and suffering, reduced for fault, and capped by the
at-fault driver's insurance.** Pain and suffering is usually your economic damages
times 1.5 to 5, based on injury severity. So $20,000 in damages with a moderate
injury (3x) points to about **$80,000** total, if there is enough coverage to pay it.
</Panel>

<Callout variant="info" title="The policy limit is the ceiling">
No matter how the math comes out, a settlement rarely exceeds the at-fault driver's
insurance policy limit, because that is the money on the table. Enter it in the
Advanced tab and the calculator caps your estimate there.
</Callout>

## How a car accident settlement is calculated

Add up your economic damages, estimate pain and suffering with a multiplier set by
severity, reduce for any share of fault, then cap at the available coverage.

<Formula formula="Settlement = (medical + lost wages + vehicle damage + pain & suffering) x (1 - fault), capped at policy limit" example={<>Example: ($20,000 economic + $60,000 pain and suffering) with no fault = <strong>$80,000</strong></>} />

| Injury severity | Multiplier | Example injuries | Typical recovery |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Minor | 1.5x | Whiplash, sprains, bruises | Weeks |
| Moderate | 3x | Broken bones, concussion, herniation without surgery | A few months |
| Severe | 4x | Surgery, major fractures, lasting impairment | 6 to 12+ months |
| Catastrophic | 5x+ | Brain injury, paralysis, amputation | Lifetime |

## Insurance policy limits: the real ceiling

This is what makes car accidents different from other injury claims. The at-fault
driver's coverage caps what you can collect directly, and coverage varies a lot by
who hit you.

| Who hit you | Typical liability coverage |
| --- | --- |
| Minimum-coverage driver | $25,000 to $50,000 per person |
| Typical personal policy | $50,000 to $100,000 |
| Rideshare (Uber / Lyft) during a trip | $1,000,000 |
| Truck / 18-wheeler (commercial) | $750,000 to $5,000,000+ |

<Callout variant="warn" title="When your damages exceed the policy">
If your losses are larger than the policy, the extra does not vanish, but it gets
harder to collect. The usual options are the driver's personal assets, an umbrella
policy, or your own underinsured-motorist (UIM) coverage. This is exactly why truck
and rideshare crashes tend to settle higher: the available coverage is far larger.
</Callout>

## What a car accident is worth, by injury

There is no true "average," because the injury drives everything. These are rough
ranges that show up across claims, not promises.

| Injury | Rough settlement range | Usual tier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Whiplash / soft tissue | $8,000 to $30,000 | Minor |
| Concussion / mild TBI | $15,000 to $100,000 | Moderate |
| Broken bones (with surgery) | $48,000 to $200,000+ | Moderate to severe |
| Herniated disc (surgery) | $80,000 to $350,000+ | Severe |
| Moderate to severe TBI | $200,000 to $5M+ | Catastrophic |
| Spinal cord injury | $1M to $25M+ | Catastrophic |

<Callout variant="info" title="For scale">
Most car accident claims are modest. Per the Insurance Information Institute, the
average auto bodily-injury liability claim was about **$26,500** in 2022. The large
numbers above come from serious, well-documented injuries with real long-term costs.
</Callout>

## Worked examples

Each uses the same formula as the calculator above, with no fault deducted.

<Example title="Rear-end whiplash, minor injury" badge="Not at fault">
$8,000 medical + $2,000 lost wages = $10,000 economic. Minor severity (1.5x) adds
$15,000 in pain and suffering. Estimated settlement: **$18,750 to $31,250**.
</Example>

<Example title="T-bone collision, broken bones, moderate injury" badge="Not at fault">
$25,000 medical + $10,000 lost wages + $5,000 vehicle damage = $40,000 economic.
Moderate (3x) adds $120,000. Estimated settlement: **$120,000 to $200,000**.
</Example>

<Example title="Severe injury, but a small policy" badge="Policy limit caps it">
$60,000 medical + $20,000 lost wages = $80,000 economic. Severe (4x) points to a
$300,000 to $500,000 settlement. But if the at-fault driver only carries a $250,000
policy, the practical recovery **caps near $250,000**, unless there is other
coverage to pursue.
</Example>

## How your state changes the number

Your share of fault reduces the settlement, but the rule depends on the state. The
calculator applies the right one once you pick your state.

| Rule | What it does | Example states |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Contributory negligence | Any fault of yours can bar recovery entirely | AL, MD, NC, VA, DC |
| Modified comparative | Reduced by your share, barred once you are about half at fault | Most states |
| Pure comparative | Reduced by your share, with no cutoff | CA, NY, FL, WA, and others |

Twelve states are also **no-fault**: your own insurance pays first through PIP, and
you can only claim pain and suffering if the injury crosses the state threshold. For
a minor injury in a no-fault state (FL, HI, KS, MA, MI, MN, NY, ND, UT, and the
choice states KY, NJ, PA), the calculator drops the pain-and-suffering portion,
since it usually cannot be claimed.

## What raises and lowers your settlement

| Raises your settlement | Lowers your settlement |
| --- | --- |
| A commercial vehicle with high coverage | A minimum-coverage or uninsured driver |
| Clear liability, e.g. you were rear-ended | Shared or disputed fault |
| Consistent treatment and strong records | Gaps in treatment, thin records |
| A permanent or surgical injury | A quick, full recovery |
| A real effect on work and daily life | A relevant pre-existing condition |

## What to do after a crash

The size of your claim follows the evidence you gather early.

<Step n={1} title="Get medical care right away">
See a doctor even if you feel fine. A gap between the crash and treatment is the
first thing an insurer uses to argue you were not really hurt.
</Step>

<Step n={2} title="Document the scene and your injuries">
Photos of the vehicles, the scene, and your injuries over time. Get the police
report number and the other driver's insurance details.
</Step>

<Step n={3} title="Find the policy limits">
Ask for the at-fault driver's liability limit, and check your own policy for
underinsured-motorist coverage. It changes what is realistically collectible.
</Step>

<Step n={4} title="Keep a pain journal">
Short, dated notes on pain, sleep, and activities you had to skip. Specific entries
are hard for an insurer to dismiss.
</Step>

<Step n={5} title="Do not rush the first offer">
Insurers open low. Knowing your own range, and having the documentation to back it
up, is what moves the number toward fair.
</Step>
