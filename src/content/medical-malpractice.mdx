<Panel>
**A medical malpractice settlement is the additional economic harm the negligence
caused (extra medical care, lost wages, and caregiver or life-care costs) plus a
non-economic amount set by how serious that added harm is.** The cost of treating
the condition you already had is excluded. So $60,000 in added economic damages
from a moderate misdiagnosis (about 3.3x) adds roughly $198,000 in non-economic
damages, for about **$258,000** before the low-to-high range and attorney fees.
</Panel>

<Callout variant="info" title="Only the additional harm counts">
You cannot recover the cost of treating the illness or injury you already had. The
provider did not cause your underlying condition, so the money you would have spent
anyway is not part of the claim. Only the extra harm the negligence caused is
compensable. That single idea is what makes malpractice math different.
</Callout>

## How a medical malpractice settlement is calculated

Add up the additional economic damages the negligence caused, multiply by a factor
set by severity and the type of malpractice, then apply a low-to-high range and
subtract the attorney fee.

<Formula formula="Settlement = added economic + (added economic x severity/type multiplier)" example={<>Example: $60,000 added economic + ($60,000 x 3.3 = $198,000 non-economic) = <strong>$258,000</strong>, then a 0.75 to 1.25 range</>} />

| Severity of the added harm | Multiplier | Typical picture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minor | 1.5x | Extra treatment, full recovery expected |
| Moderate | 3x | Lasting added effects |
| Severe | 4.5x | Added surgery, long-term disability |
| Catastrophic | 6x | Permanent injury, lifelong care |

The type of malpractice adds a smaller bump, and lasting harm adds a little more.

| Type of malpractice | Bump | Duration of added harm | Bump |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Birth injury | +1.0 | 12 months or more | +0.25 |
| Anesthesia error | +0.5 | 24 months or more | +0.5 |
| Surgical error, misdiagnosis, failure to treat | +0.3 | | |
| Medication error, ER error, hospital negligence | +0.2 | | |

The combined multiplier is held between 1.5 and 8.

## Why only the additional harm counts (causation)

Causation is the part of a malpractice case that surprises people, and it is the
reason two patients with the same final condition can have very different claims.
The law asks a narrow question: what extra harm did the negligence cause, beyond
the illness or injury the patient already had?

<Callout variant="warn" title="You cannot recover the cost of the illness you already had">
If you needed an appendectomy anyway, the cost of that surgery is not part of your
claim. If a surgical error then caused an infection that added $50,000 in care and
kept you out of work, that added harm is what you recover. Enter only the extra
cost the negligence caused, and use the original-cost field to see it excluded for
contrast.
</Callout>

This is also why proving a malpractice case is expensive: it takes medical experts
to separate the harm the negligence caused from the natural course of the original
condition. The stronger that causation story, the higher the realistic settlement.

## Average medical malpractice settlement amounts

There is no reliable single average, because the added harm drives everything.
These are rough framed ranges that show up across claims, not promises, and any
specific case can fall well outside them.

| Added harm | Rough settlement range |
| --- | --- |
| Minor, short-term added harm | $30,000 to $150,000 |
| Lasting injury, added surgery | $150,000 to $600,000 |
| Severe, long-term disability | $500,000 to $2M |
| Catastrophic or lifelong care | $1M to $10M+ |

## Medical malpractice damage caps by state

Most states cap non-economic (pain and suffering) damages in medical malpractice
cases specifically, even states that do not cap ordinary injury cases. Economic
damages (the actual bills and lost income) are usually not capped.

These caps change most years, several index to inflation, and some have been struck
down by state supreme courts, so hard-coding a dollar figure would be misleading.
The calculator flags when your state has a cap and points you to verify the current
number. Confirm the live figure and whether it survives current litigation with a
malpractice attorney in your state.

## Birth injury, surgical error, and misdiagnosis

The type of malpractice shifts the value, and birth injury sits at the top. A birth
injury such as cerebral palsy or a brachial plexus injury usually means decades of
therapy, equipment, and lost earning capacity across a full lifetime, so the
life-care and caregiver costs dwarf everything else. That is why birth injury
carries the highest multiplier and the largest settlements of any type.

Surgical errors and misdiagnosis are the most common claims. A missed cancer
diagnosis that let the disease progress, or a wrong-site or retained-instrument
surgical error, can produce severe added harm even when the original condition was
treatable. When malpractice causes a death, the claim usually becomes a wrongful
death case, and you can estimate that separately with the
[wrongful death settlement calculator](/wrongful-death-settlement-calculator).

## What you actually take home after attorney fees

The range the calculator shows is the gross settlement. What reaches you is lower,
because malpractice cases are taken on contingency and case costs come out of the
recovery.

Med-mal contingency fees commonly run 33 to 40%, and a handful of states cap the
fee by statute, often on a sliding scale that shrinks the percentage as the
recovery grows. Expert witnesses, who are essential in these cases, are a large
separate cost. Enter your fee percentage in Advanced and the calculator shows the
estimated net range after that fee.

<Example title="Missed diagnosis, moderate added harm" badge="Misdiagnosis">
A patient needed a $20,000 procedure anyway (excluded). A delayed diagnosis added

</Example>

<Example title="Surgical error, severe, 18 months of added harm" badge="Net after 40% fee">
$100,000 added medical + $40,000 lost wages + $60,000 caregiver costs = $200,000

5.05x multiplier, adding $1,010,000, for $1,210,000 gross ($907,500 to $1,512,500).

</Example>

<Example title="Birth injury needing lifelong care" badge="Net after 33% fee">
$250,000 added medical + $750,000 in caregiver and life-care costs = $1,000,000

7.5x multiplier, adding $7,500,000, for $8,500,000 gross ($6,375,000 to

</Example>

## What to do in a suspected malpractice case

The strength of your claim follows the causation evidence and the deadlines you
protect early.

<Step n={1} title="Get your complete medical records">
Request the full record, including imaging and notes, from every provider. The case
lives or dies on what the records show about the original condition and the added
harm.
</Step>

<Step n={2} title="Get a second opinion and stabilize your health">
See another provider to treat the added harm and to document, in a new record, what
went wrong. Your recovery comes first, and it also builds the file.
</Step>

<Step n={3} title="Watch the statute of limitations">
Malpractice deadlines are short and vary by state, sometimes running from when the
harm was discovered. Missing it usually ends the claim, so confirm your window
early.
</Step>

<Step n={4} title="Separate the added harm from the original condition">
List the extra bills, lost income, and future care caused by the negligence, apart
from what your original condition would have cost. That difference is your claim.
</Step>

<Step n={5} title="Talk to a malpractice attorney before settling">
These cases need medical experts and rarely settle fairly without one. Knowing your
own range, and whether your state caps damages or fees, is what keeps a low offer
from looking reasonable.
</Step>
