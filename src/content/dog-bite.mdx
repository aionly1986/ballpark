<Panel>
**A dog bite settlement is your economic damages (medical bills, future care, and
lost earnings) plus a pain-and-suffering amount set by how serious the injury is,
adjusted for liability.** Pain and suffering is your economic damages times a factor
of about 1.5 for a minor bite up to 8 for permanent disfigurement. So $10,000 in
damages with a moderate bite adds about $30,000 in pain and suffering, for roughly
**$40,000** total, before any provocation or state-rule adjustment.
</Panel>

<Callout variant="info" title="Scarring and facial injuries drive the value">
Unlike most injury claims, a dog bite is often about permanent marks, not just
medical cost. A visible facial scar, nerve damage, or a bite to a young child can
push the value far above what the medical bills alone would suggest.
</Callout>

## How a dog bite settlement is calculated

Add up your economic damages, multiply by a factor set by severity and aggravating
factors, then adjust for liability (your state rule, plus provocation or
trespassing).

<Formula formula="Settlement = (economic + economic x severity factor) x liability" example={<>Example: $10,000 economic + ($10,000 x 3 = $30,000 pain and suffering) = <strong>$40,000</strong>, then x 1.0 in a strict-liability state</>} />

| Bite severity | Factor | Typical injuries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Minor | 1.5x | Shallow bite, no stitches, quick recovery |
| Moderate | 3x | Punctures needing stitches, some lasting mark |
| Severe | 4.5x | Deep lacerations, surgery, tissue damage |
| Disfiguring | 6x+ | Permanent scarring, facial reconstruction |

Facial injury, permanent scarring, nerve damage, infection, emotional trauma, and a
child victim each raise the factor further, up to a cap of 8.

## Strict liability vs the one-bite rule

This is what makes state law matter for dog bites. Most states hold the owner
responsible even if the dog never bit anyone before. A minority follow the one-bite
rule, where you usually must show the owner knew the dog was dangerous.

| Rule | What you must show | Example states |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Strict liability | Little more than the bite and your damages | CA, FL, IL, MI, PA, and most states |
| One-bite rule | The owner knew, or should have known, the dog was dangerous | AR, ID, KS, MS, NV, NM, NY, ND, OR, SD, TX, VA, WY |

<Callout variant="warn" title="Provocation and trespassing are defenses">
If you provoked or teased the dog, or were somewhere you were not allowed to be, the
value drops sharply and in some states the claim can fail entirely. The calculator
lowers the estimate when either applies.
</Callout>

## What a dog bite is worth, by injury

There is no true average, because the injury drives everything. These are rough
ranges that show up across claims, not promises.

| Injury | Rough settlement range |
| --- | --- |
| Minor puncture, no scar | $3,000 to $15,000 |
| Puncture with stitches | $10,000 to $50,000 |
| Deep laceration or surgery | $30,000 to $150,000 |
| Permanent facial scarring | $75,000 to $300,000+ |
| Nerve damage or major disfigurement | $100,000 to $1M+ |

<Callout variant="info" title="A child changes the picture">
Children are bitten on the face and neck far more often than adults, and juries
treat childhood scarring seriously. A facial scar on a young child is one of the
highest-value dog-bite scenarios, even when the medical bills are modest.
</Callout>

## Worked examples

Each uses the same formula as the calculator above.

<Example title="Moderate bite to the leg, strict-liability state" badge="Not provoked">
$6,000 medical + $1,000 lost earnings = $7,000 economic. Moderate (3x) points to
$21,000 in general damages. Estimated settlement: **$21,000 to $35,000**.
</Example>

<Example title="Facial scar on a child" badge="Disfiguring">
$15,000 medical + $10,000 future care = $25,000 economic. Disfiguring, plus facial,
scarring, and child factors, caps the multiplier at 8. Estimated settlement:
**$169,000 to $281,000**.
</Example>

<Example title="Same bite, but a one-bite state with no prior history" badge="Liability reduced">
The same $7,000 moderate bite in a one-bite state, where the owner had no reason to
know the dog was dangerous, cuts liability in half. Estimated settlement:
**$10,500 to $17,500**.
</Example>

## Who pays a dog bite claim

Most dog-bite claims are paid by the owner's homeowner or renter insurance, not out
of the owner's pocket. Those policies commonly carry $100,000 to $300,000 in
liability coverage, which is usually the practical ceiling on your recovery.

| Source | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| Homeowner / renter insurance | The usual payer. Check the policy limit. |
| Umbrella policy | Extra coverage above the base policy, if the owner has one. |
| Owner personal assets | Where you look if there is no policy, and harder to collect. |

## What to do after a dog bite

The size of your claim follows the evidence you gather early.

<Step n={1} title="Get medical care and a rabies assessment">
See a doctor the same day. Bites get infected, and a treatment record started right
away is the foundation of the claim.
</Step>

<Step n={2} title="Photograph the wound over time">
Take photos immediately and again as it heals, especially if a scar forms. Scarring
is the single biggest driver of value.
</Step>

<Step n={3} title="Identify the dog and owner">
Get the owner's name, address, and insurance, and the dog's vaccination history.
Report the bite to animal control, which creates an official record.
</Step>

<Step n={4} title="Find the homeowner policy">
Ask the owner for their homeowner or renter insurer. That is who realistically pays,
and the policy limit shapes what is collectible.
</Step>

<Step n={5} title="Do not accept a quick offer">
Insurers open low, often before a scar has fully formed. Knowing your own range, and
documenting the injury, is what moves the number toward fair.
</Step>
