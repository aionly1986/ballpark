<Panel>
**A herniated disc settlement is your economic damages (medical bills, future care,
and lost income) plus a pain-and-suffering amount driven by two things: how many
discs are involved, and whether a surgeon operated.** A single herniated disc treated
with therapy carries a 3x factor. Two discs fused, with a permanent restriction,
reaches 6x.
</Panel>

<Callout variant="info" title="Surgery matters more than the disc">
Two people can have the identical MRI and settle for wildly different amounts. What
separates them is almost never which disc herniated. It is whether the records show an
operation, because surgery is objective, documented, and expensive.
</Callout>

## How a herniated disc settlement is calculated

<Formula formula="Settlement = economic + (economic x multiplier), reduced for fault" example={<>Example: $20,000 economic + ($20,000 x 3 = $60,000 pain and suffering) = <strong>$80,000</strong> for a single herniated disc treated without surgery</>} />

| Disc injury | Base multiplier |
| --- | --- |
| Bulging disc (contained) | 2.5x |
| Herniated disc (ruptured) | 3x |

| Treatment | Added |
| --- | --- |
| No surgery (therapy, chiropractic, medication) | none |
| Injections (epidural, radiofrequency ablation) | +0.5 |
| Discectomy | +1.0 |
| Single-level fusion | +1.5 |
| Multi-level fusion | +2.0 |

A second affected disc adds **0.5**, a third adds **0.75**, and a permanent impairment
adds another **0.5**.

## How much is a 2 herniated disc settlement?

This is the most-asked question about disc claims, and the answer is that the second
disc helps less than people hope, while the surgery helps more than they expect.

Holding economic damages constant at **$20,000** for **two herniated discs**, here is
what the calculator above actually produces:

| Treatment (two discs) | Multiplier | Estimated range |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No surgery | 3.5x | $67,500 to $112,500 |
| Injections | 4x | $75,000 to $125,000 |
| Discectomy | 4.5x | $82,500 to $137,500 |
| Single-level fusion | 5x | $90,000 to $150,000 |
| Multi-level fusion | 5.5x | $97,500 to $162,500 |

<Callout variant="info" title="A multi-level fusion always counts as at least two discs">
By definition it fuses more than one. The calculator enforces that, so you cannot pick
a multi-level fusion and one disc and get a lower number than the procedure implies.
</Callout>

## Herniated disc settlement without surgery

A disc treated conservatively settles for meaningfully less than the same disc that
needed an operation. That is not a judgment about your pain. It is a reflection of
what an adjuster can see in the file.

| Discs | No surgery | With a single-level fusion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One disc | $60,000 to $100,000 | $82,500 to $137,500 |
| Two discs | $67,500 to $112,500 | $90,000 to $150,000 |

<Callout variant="warn" title="Do not settle before you know if you need surgery">
This is the costly mistake. Settling while surgery is still a possibility forfeits the
single largest driver of value in a disc claim, and once you sign you cannot reopen it.
</Callout>

## Bulging disc vs herniated disc

| | Bulging disc | Herniated disc |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it is | The disc protrudes but the outer wall is intact | The outer wall tears and the inner material escapes |
| Base multiplier | 2.5x | 3x |
| Insurer argument | "Common and degenerative" | Harder to dismiss, but still fought |

The gap between them is smaller than the gap between surgery and no surgery. A bulging
disc that required a fusion is worth more than a herniated disc treated with therapy.

## C4-C5, C5-C6, C6-C7, L4-L5, and L5-S1 settlements

People search for their exact vertebrae, so it is worth being direct: **the specific
level matters much less than what was done about it.** A C5-C6 herniation treated with
therapy is worth less than a C6-C7 herniation that needed a fusion, and the reverse is
equally true.

| Region | Common levels | What tends to happen there |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cervical (neck) | C4-C5, C5-C6, C6-C7 | Arm pain, numbness, cervical fusion or discectomy |
| Lumbar (lower back) | L4-L5, L5-S1 | Sciatica, leg weakness, microdiscectomy or fusion |

That is why this calculator asks how many discs are affected rather than which
vertebrae. A two-disc fusion is worth more than a single-disc fusion at any level.

## The pre-existing degeneration argument

<Callout variant="warn" title="Expect the insurer to blame your age">
Disc degeneration is visible on the imaging of most adults, and insurers use that to
argue your spine was already bad. The counter is that a defendant takes the victim as
they find them: if the crash made an existing condition symptomatic, that aggravation
is compensable. Records showing you were working and functioning normally beforehand
are what carry the argument.
</Callout>

## Worked examples

Each uses the same formula as the calculator above, with no fault deducted.

<Example title="One herniated disc, physical therapy only" badge="No surgery">
$15,000 medical + $5,000 lost wages = $20,000 economic. Herniated disc (3x) adds
$60,000. Estimated settlement: **$60,000 to $100,000**.
</Example>

<Example title="Two herniated discs, still no surgery" badge="Two discs">
Same $20,000 economic. Two discs lift the multiplier to 3.5x, adding $70,000.
Estimated settlement: **$67,500 to $112,500**.
</Example>

<Example title="Two-disc fusion with a permanent restriction" badge="Surgery">
$45,000 medical + $15,000 lost income = $60,000 economic. Herniated disc, multi-level
fusion, two discs, and permanence reach a 6x multiplier, adding $360,000. Estimated
settlement: **$315,000 to $525,000**.
</Example>
